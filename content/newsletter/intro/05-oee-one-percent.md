What would a 1% improvement in OEE be worth to your business?

Most people have never worked it out. For most manufacturers, it's a very significant number.

## How the calculator works

You need two figures from the annual report: revenue and gross profit. Pick your industry, and add anything else you know to make the estimate sharper, such as how much you make yourself, how much extra output you could sell, your margin and your materials costs. Everything has a sensible default if you don't know it.

Then move the slider. It starts at 1% and goes up to 25%.

You don't need to know your OEE. Here, 1% means 1% more good output from the same equipment and hours, which is deliberately cautious. At 50% OEE, one extra point of OEE is actually 2% more output.

The improvement needs to land on the equipment that limits your output. Gains on a machine that's waiting for the one before it don't add output.

## Why it matters

Successful MES/MOM programmes often deliver a 10% to 20% improvement in production output. Deloitte's 2025 survey of large manufacturers found the same range.

Even a much smaller improvement often pays for the whole project, and more, within 12 to 24 months. And that's before the savings the calculator doesn't count, like less scrap and rework, lower inventory, faster traceability and lower energy use per unit.

Put your number in the first paragraph of your business case, before anyone sees a software cost.
