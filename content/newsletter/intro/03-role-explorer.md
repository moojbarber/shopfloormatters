Everyone in a factory touches the same orders and the same machines. They just ask very different questions of them.

A CEO thinks in years and quarters: capacity, margin, customer service and reputation. An operator thinks in minutes: is this the right material, and is this part good?

An MES/MOM programme touches all of them. If you only make the case in one of those languages, you lose the rest of the room.

## What the role explorer shows

The role explorer on the site covers 21 roles across four levels, from the boardroom to the shopfloor. Pick one and you'll see:

- What that person is measured on, and the time horizon they work to
- What gets in the way of their day
- The shopfloor applications that make the biggest difference to them, and why
- What changes once operations are connected
- How to make the case to them

The CEO page, for example, suggests talking about growth and risk rather than software, and showing what hidden capacity is worth in revenue.

## Four ways to use it

1. **Before a steering committee or board paper,** look up each person in the room and frame your points around what they're measured on.
2. **When you're building the business case,** check that every role's benefit is in there, not just production's.
3. **Share it with colleagues** in quality, maintenance or IT, so they can see what's in it for them.
4. **If you're moving into operations from IT,** or the other way round, use it to see what your new colleagues care about.
